# Support Escalation Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines situations where a customer support case should be transferred to a senior support representative or specialized team.

## Standard Support Cases

Customer support representatives should resolve routine questions using approved policies and troubleshooting procedures.

Examples include:

- Shipping status questions.
- Standard return requests.
- Basic product troubleshooting.
- Order cancellation requests.
- General billing questions.

## When to Escalate

A case should be escalated when:

- The issue cannot be resolved using approved procedures.
- The customer reports a potential safety issue.
- A customer disputes a significant financial transaction.
- There is a suspected security incident.
- The customer has experienced repeated product failures.
- The case involves a legal or regulatory concern.
- A policy exception may be required.
- The customer has already contacted support multiple times without resolution.

## Safety-Related Issues

Safety-related cases should receive priority escalation.

Examples include:

- Smoke from a product.
- Burning smell.
- Sparks.
- Electrical shock.
- Swollen battery.
- Product overheating to an unsafe level.
- Physical damage that creates a potential safety hazard.

Support representatives should not provide instructions that could expose the customer to additional danger.

## Security Incidents

Cases involving suspected unauthorized account access, stolen credentials, or unauthorized transactions should be escalated to the appropriate security process.

Support should avoid requesting unnecessary sensitive information.

## Financial Disputes

High-value billing disputes or suspected duplicate charges should be escalated when they cannot be resolved through normal billing procedures.

The case should contain the order number, transaction information, and a description of the issue.

## Repeated Failures

A customer reporting the same unresolved product issue multiple times should be escalated for additional review.

The case history should include previous troubleshooting steps and previous support interactions.

## Policy Exceptions

Support representatives should not promise an exception before approval.

When an exception may be appropriate, the case should be escalated with:

- Customer request.
- Relevant order information.
- Applicable policy.
- Reason an exception is being requested.
- Previous actions taken by support.

## Escalation Priority

### Critical

Immediate escalation is required for:

- Safety incidents.
- Suspected security breaches.
- Serious regulatory or legal concerns.

### High

Priority escalation should be considered for:

- Significant financial disputes.
- Repeated unresolved failures.
- Major service-impacting issues.

### Normal

Standard escalation may be used for:

- Complex policy questions.
- Requests requiring manager approval.
- Cases where normal troubleshooting has failed.

## Escalation Documentation

Every escalated case should contain enough information for the receiving team to understand the problem without asking the customer to repeat basic information.

The support representative should document:

- Customer issue.
- Order number.
- Product information.
- Relevant dates.
- Troubleshooting already performed.
- Policy involved.
- Reason for escalation.

## Customer Communication

Support representatives should not promise a specific resolution unless the resolution has been approved.

Customers should be informed that the case has been escalated and given an expected follow-up timeframe when one is available.ty
